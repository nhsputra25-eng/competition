02 / ProblemWhat We See Every Day

Piling Up

One household can throw away about one plastic bag every day. Multiply that by every house in your neighborhood.

Hard To Break Down

Plastic bottles can take hundreds of years to break down. In the ocean, they break into tiny pieces that marine life can mistake for food.

Hard To Sort

Recycling points exist, but many people don't bother sorting their waste—often because it's inconvenient or takes too much time.

03 / ProcessFour Simple Steps

01

Discover

Look around and find the problem that shows up most often near your home, school, or boarding house.

02

Think

Come up with ideas. They don't need to be fancy. They just need to be something you can actually try.

03

Build

Make the smallest version possible. A rough prototype is far more useful than a perfect plan in your head.

04

Impact

See what changes, even if it's small. If it works, repeat it. If it doesn't, go back and adjust it.

04 / SolutionsSmall Ideas We're Trying

Digital Waste Bank

A simple way to record, sort and track plastic waste. Points can be exchanged for everyday essentials.

Mini Composter

No yard space? A small bin with dry leaves can turn organic waste into compost in about six weeks.

Plastic Upcycling

Old plastic packaging can be turned into useful items. Small efforts like this help keep plastic out of landfills.

About

We Started With
One Street.

There was a pile of plastic waste on our street that never seemed to end. Every week it was cleaned up, and every week it came back. We got tired of just complaining—so we started taking notes.

What We Noticed

For the first three months, we sat at the neighborhood watch post every Saturday morning. We counted plastic bottles, tracked when people threw away their trash, and asked our neighbors why they didn't sort it. The answer wasn't laziness—it was that there was no convenient place to sort waste, and there wasn't enough reason for people to take the extra effort.

From those notes, we realized the problem wasn't people—it was the system. That was the starting point for everything on this site.

Who We Are

We're a small team of three: one who likes working with data, one who likes making things, and one who enjoys talking to people. We're around 19 years old. We're not a startup or a big community—we're just people who live on the same street.

What We've Done So Far

  • 2023 – First field notes, with 12 households tracked.
  • 2024 – First version of a digital waste bank tested in one neighborhood block.
  • 2025 – Two nearby blocks asked to try the same system.
  • 2026 – A small composting idea for boarding houses was tested in two rental homes.
